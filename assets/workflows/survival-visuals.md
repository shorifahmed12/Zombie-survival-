# Visual workflow for original survival shorts

Start each scene with a silent question the viewer can answer by looking. For a ten-second test, use one location, one change in the environment, and one choice by the survivor.

## Building the shot

1. Draw the location in three removable layers. A distant skyline gives the eye a horizon. A midground landmark fixes the survivor's position. A nearby shape can hide part of the frame and reveal movement. Use the strongest light-dark edge where the viewer should look.
2. Stage a wide shot with enough space to see where the survivor can go. When the viewer needs new information, cut to the smallest detail that explains it. Return to the wide with the landmark on the same side so the consequence is easy to read.
3. Give the rig a readable silhouette at phone size. For a reaction, turn the eyes or head toward the cue, hold briefly, then let the shoulders and weight follow. For distant figures, use uneven gait and spacing so several walkers feel distinct.
4. Give an important prop two drawings if needed, one readable in silhouette and one for an insert. Use a moving needle, changing light or swinging latch to tell the story without paragraphs of screen text.

## Reusable Remotion pieces

Keep original sky, skyline, landmark and foreground art separate. Give the character rig pose, facing and gaze controls. Keep prop states and lighting accents independent of the character. Reuse these pieces across shots by changing framing, scale and timing. Keep all layers on the same frame clock with useCurrentFrame(). Use Sequence for each shot and interpolate() with clamped ends for small camera moves. Store timing in seconds and convert to frames using the composition fps.

## Original ten-second blocking exercise

| Time | Framing | Silent action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–3 s | Wide, rain-blue pump station | A survivor waits on a high walkway. A hanging work lamp reveals empty steps underneath. |
| 3–5 s | Insert, same light direction | A wet handprint forms on the underside of the translucent stair tread. |
| 5–7 s | Medium | The survivor's eyes drop first. Their raised boot hangs in the air before it slowly draws back. |
| 7–10 s | Return to wide | The survivor backs away. The swinging lamp reveals a second print ahead of them. Hold on that discovery. |

Build the still poses first. Play the sequence muted at phone size. If the handprint is easy to miss, enlarge the insert or give it more time before adding effects. Use original artwork, character design, props and staging for each episode.
